SnapSort offers several **placement modes**, or simply **modes**,
which alter how the items and ghosts behave while they are dragged.

## Euclidean

Euclidean is the default mode (`mode: "euclidean"`, or omit `mode`). It
implements a nearest-neighbor algorithm that tries to place an item
at a location that is geometrically closest to where the pointer is
currently positioned.

<EuclideanPlacementDemo />

Euclidean placement is arguably the simplest and most intuitive,
making it suitable for common layouts such as kanban boards,
vertical lists, etc.

## Insertion

Insertion placement (`mode: "insertion"`) uses a special ghost that
levitates over the gap where the item will be inserted into.
The original items remain in their original positions until the drop commits.

<InsertionPlacementDemo />

Insertion mode has relatively less visual clutter during drag,
so it is ideal for large lists or lists with deep level of nested containers,
like a file explorer.

## Swap

Swap placement (`mode: "swap"`) keeps the dragged item in its original
position while a ghost follows the pointer. The item is placed over whatever
other item the pointer was hovering over when it was let go.

<SwapPlacementDemo />

In some sense, you can say swap is a hybrid between euclidean and insertion modes.
It's ideal for large grid-like layouts where a ghost following the pointer
in real-time may produce too much visual noise.

## Progressive

Progressive placement (`mode: "progressive"`) is designed for layouts
that "progress" from top left to bottom right.

Compare the two demos above. The difference is most noticeable if you take an
item from the top row and move it around near the bottom right of the second row.
In the euclidean version, the item and its ghost can jump back to the top row, because
mathematically speaking that is the closest position.
Meanwhile in progressive mode, once an item is placed near the second row,
it only places items in the second row.

In case you haven't caught on by now, this mode is perfect for recreating
the [menacing green owl app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duolingo).
